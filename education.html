§ 02 — Education

Learning to think like an owner.

Our education program takes members with no finance background to writing a full five-year investment thesis in one semester — and keeps building from there.

New member curriculum

A twelve-week foundation.

Weekly sessions are led by senior members and combine short lectures with hands-on case work on real companies. Each module ends with a short exercise that builds toward the final pitch.

Week 1

The case for compounding

Why long horizons win: the math of compounding, the cost of turnover and taxes, and the behavioral edge of patience.

Week 2

Reading the three statements

Income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement — and how they connect. Practice on a real 10-K.

Week 3

Returns on capital

ROIC, ROE, and free cash flow conversion: how to tell a business that creates value from one that consumes it.

Week 4

Competitive advantage

Moats in practice — brands, network effects, switching costs, scale — and how they strengthen or decay over time.

Week 5

Industry & competitive analysis

Porter’s Five Forces, industry structure, and why some industries reliably produce compounders.

Week 6

Management & capital allocation

Buybacks, dividends, M&A, and reinvestment. Reading shareholder letters and proxy statements critically.

Week 7

Valuation I: intrinsic value

Owner earnings and discounted cash flow models built for a five-to-ten-year view.

Week 8

Valuation II: expectations

Reverse DCF and multiples — understanding what the market is already pricing in.

Week 9

Risk & margin of safety

Pre-mortems, downside scenarios, balance-sheet stress tests, and position sizing.

Week 10

Behavioral finance

Common biases, the discipline of holding through volatility, and keeping an investment journal.

Week 11

Writing the memo

How to structure a clear, honest investment thesis — including what would make you wrong.

Week 12

Final pitch

New members present a full long-term pitch to the fund and receive feedback from senior members.

Beyond the first semester

How members keep compounding.

After the core curriculum, members join pitch teams and take on progressively more responsibility in the fund.

01

Analyst track

Join a pitch team, co-author memos, and own the ongoing monitoring of a portfolio holding.

02

Book club

A monthly discussion of a classic investing book, shareholder letter, or case study.

03

Annual letter

Each year the fund publishes a letter reviewing every holding against its original thesis — mistakes included.

04

Speaker series

Conversations with long-term investors, operators, and alumni about how they think about durable businesses.

05

Mentorship

Every new member is paired with a senior member for research guidance and career advice.

06

Recruiting prep

Technical and behavioral interview prep, stock-pitch practice, and résumé reviews.
