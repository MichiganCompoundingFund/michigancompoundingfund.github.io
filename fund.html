§ 01 — The Fund

Our strategy.

A concentrated, long-only portfolio of high-quality public companies, bought at reasonable prices and held for five years or more.

Investment approach

Underwrite the business, not the stock chart.

Every MCF position starts with one question: will this business be meaningfully more valuable in five years than it is today — and are we paying a fair price for that future?

We look for companies that can reinvest capital at high returns, are protected by durable competitive advantages, and are run by management teams who think like owners. We avoid short-term trading, leverage, and options. When we buy, we expect to hold.

  • 01

    Durable moat

    Brand, network effects, switching costs, scale, or cost advantages that protect returns on capital from competition.

  • 02

    Reinvestment runway

    Room to deploy more capital at attractive returns — the engine that turns a good business into a compounder.

  • 03

    Aligned management

    Leaders with a record of sensible capital allocation, meaningful ownership, and candid communication with shareholders.

  • 04

    Balance-sheet strength

    Conservative financing so the company can survive downturns — and ideally take advantage of them.

  • 05

    Margin of safety

    A purchase price that leaves room for error, based on conservative assumptions about growth and margins.

Investment process

From idea to ownership.

Each idea passes through the same disciplined steps, so that every holding in the portfolio has been researched, challenged, and documented.

Screen & source

Ideas come from screens for high returns on capital, annual letters, industry deep-dives, and member curiosity.

Deep research

Pitch teams read filings, build a model, study competitors, and map the key drivers of value over five years.

Investment memo

A written thesis covering the business, moat, management, valuation, risks, and what would prove us wrong.

Pitch & vote

The team pitches to the full fund. Members challenge the thesis; the investment committee votes.

Own & monitor

Holdings are reviewed against the original thesis each year. We sell when the thesis breaks, not when the price wobbles.

Sell discipline

We sell when the thesis changes — not the headlines.

A position is trimmed or exited when the competitive advantage erodes, management makes poor capital-allocation decisions, the valuation becomes extreme relative to our conservative estimate of value, or a clearly better long-term opportunity emerges.
